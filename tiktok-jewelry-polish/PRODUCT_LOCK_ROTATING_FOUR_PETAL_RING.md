# Product Lock Card — Four-Petal Rotating Open-Band Ring

Use this card only when the user is working on the exact silver-tone ring shown in the user's uploaded reference photos. Do not transfer these features to other rings.

## Source-of-truth references
- Use the user's real front, angled, side, underside, and worn/product photos from the current ring task.
- The final close-up image showing the open band is the authoritative reference for the opening, terminal ends, and gap.
- Earlier AI-generated images are not authoritative for construction.

## Confirmed product construction
1. **Flower face:** exactly four petals. It is not a five-petal flower, six-petal flower, or generic clover.
2. **Metal:** polished silver-tone / silver-white appearance. Do not change it to gold, black, or a different finish.
3. **Stone layout:** preserve the clear central round stone, the tapered baguette-like stones radiating within the petals, and the small pavé stones around the petal edges exactly as shown. Do not invent or standardize a stone count that is not clearly visible.
4. **Two-layer head:** the ring head has an upper four-petal flower face and a distinct lower flower-shaped support/layer beneath it. Do not flatten them into one layer or remove the lower layer.
5. **Rotation:** the upper flower face can rotate left and right relative to the lower layer. Preserve this relationship. Do not depict a fixed one-piece head. Do not claim 360-degree rotation unless the user separately confirms it.
6. **Open shank:** the band is C-shaped and open. The opening is at the back of the ring near the flower base, as shown in the user's final macro reference.
7. **Open ends:** preserve the real gap width, end alignment, terminal shape, polished finish, and exact placement shown in the macro reference. Do not move the gap to the front, invent a hinge or clasp, or close the band.
8. **Overall proportions:** preserve the ring-head size, band thickness, head height, lower-layer depth, and relation between the head and shank.

## Feature infographic layout approved by the user's request
When making one detail image that explains both functions:
- The complete ring is the dominant hero and remains the largest visual element.
- Use two restrained curved arrows to indicate left-right rotation of the upper flower face.
- Place the open-band close-up in a small secondary circular or rounded-rectangle inset. It must not become a giant close-up or overpower the ring.
- A fine pointer may connect the inset to the actual opening near the back of the flower base.
- Keep the layout as one coherent detail graphic, not a collage or grid.
- Use US-English labels only. Suggested copy:
  - “Rotating Flower Top”
  - “Adjustable Open Band”
- Do not use “360° Rotation” or other unsupported claims.
- Do not put Chinese characters in the image.

## Reject these common AI errors
- Five petals instead of four.
- One-layer flower head instead of two layers.
- No visible lower flower-shaped support.
- Upper flower face fused to the lower layer or unable to rotate.
- Open band gap moved to the front or away from the flower base.
- Wrong terminal-end shape, wrong gap width, extra band ends, invented hinge/clasp, or a closed shank.
- Baguette stones replaced by round stones, missing pavé, altered center stone, or changed stone placement.
- Gold-toned metal, green cast, plastic-like metal, excessive fake sparkle, or distorted proportions.

## Quality gate
Before delivery, compare the result against the actual product photos, especially the final open-band macro. If any of the critical construction details above are wrong, do not call the result accurate. Retry once with the failed detail stated explicitly; if it still fails, use source-photo editing or ask for a clearer reference.
